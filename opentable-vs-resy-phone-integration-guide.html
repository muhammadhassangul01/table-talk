OpenTable vs Resy: phone integration setup guide

How to connect AI phone answering to either platform — and how to choose which one fits your floor.

Updated August 13, 2026 · 6 min read

If your restaurant takes reservations, you're on one of two platforms 95% of the time: OpenTable or Resy. The choice affects everything from your floor plan to your marketing data — and, increasingly, how your phone system books tables.

This guide compares the two, then walks through exactly how to connect AI phone answering to whichever one you run.

OpenTable vs Resy at a glance

What matters OpenTable Resy
Best for Large networks, established dining rooms Independent, buzz-driven restaurants
Discovery Huge search marketplace Curated, often faster hype cycles
No-show policy tools Cards, deposit options Cards, deposits, shorter windows
Fee model Subscription + per-cover Per-cover style

Both platforms are excellent. For a phone AI, the important thing isn't the platform — it's that the reservation lands in your real book, with the guest's correct name, party size, and contact info, so your host and kitchen see it instantly.

How AI phone booking works on either platform

The experience is identical whether you run OpenTable or Resy. When a guest calls:

  1. The AI greets the caller with your brand's voice and greeting.
  2. It reads availability from your live reservation calendar.
  3. It books the party under the caller's name, at the right size and time.
  4. It sends an SMS confirmation with the menu and directions.
  5. The booking appears in your OpenTable or Resy app like any other.

The pro tip

You don't have to pick. TableTalk connects to both OpenTable and Resy from one line and one dashboard — so multi-platform restaurants never lose a booking to the "other" system.

Setup guide: OpenTable

  1. Step 1 — Connect your OpenTable account. In TableTalk, authorize OpenTable with read-write booking access. No API keys, no developer.
  2. Step 2 — Turn on conditional call forwarding. From your host phone, forward calls when busy or unanswered to your TableTalk number. Your existing number stays public.
  3. Step 3 — Upload your menu and rules. Menu, hours, policies, dietary notes. The AI reads them all from your live setup.
  4. Step 4 — Pick a voice and test. Make a test call, watch the booking land in OpenTable, adjust the greeting to taste. Go live the same shift.

Setup guide: Resy

  1. Step 1 — Connect your Resy account. Authorize in TableTalk the same way — a couple of clicks, done in minutes.
  2. Step 2 — Forward your line. Conditional forwarding means TableTalk answers when you're busy, after hours, or at the door — never when a host is available.
  3. Step 3 — Set your house rules. Deposit policies, party caps, and same-night booking windows all become part of what the AI enforces.
  4. Step 4 — Test a booking end-to-end. Call in, book a table, and confirm it appears in Resy before your next service.

Which one should you choose?

  • Pick OpenTable if you want the largest discovery marketplace and you operate in a market where guests search it by default.
  • Pick Resy if you're an independent with a strong brand and want a more curated, hype-friendly booking experience.
  • Pick both if your market splits across the two — TableTalk removes the friction of maintaining separate phone setups.

Whatever you choose, the phone AI's job is the same: never let a caller who's ready to book become a missed reservation.
